# Working boat, coastal water and diver operations

September 28, 2026. Implements the designer’s playtest commission in the independent Three.js project. The updated living Bible records the explicit decisions; this document records implementation and review evidence, not a claim of complete game acceptance.

## Connected changes

Chandlery stations describe where equipment lives, not how many items a boat can own. There was no numeric slot cap in the prior purchase code: the hydraulic hauler’s rank requirement and unhelpful failed-purchase feedback made it look unavailable. The hauler is now available from day one. The shop groups equipment by purpose, states ownership/operation/requirements, explains the immediate benefit and actual before/after figures, and previews fittings on a rotatable 3D model. A working engine repower trades speed/acceleration for consumption; a compatible fuel-management package improves range. Sea consumption, passage estimates and charges use the same effective specification. All compatible systems coexist and remain attached to the purchased hull. Confirmation remains explicit and initially on Cancel.

The twelve career hulls have named presentation profiles based on the preserved artwork, including the Island Tender’s collar and motors, timber-deck sister, squared landing bow, tug fittings, and Channel Master twin hulls. All twenty-five inherited traffic identities also have explicit 3D interpretations, including the open patrol RIB, oared galley and sailing multihull. Dimensions, deck bag coordinates, collision and handling still belong to the simulation. The sea renderer and shop preview share the same model factories. Installed systems appear at physical stations; inaccessible interior systems have a location highlight in the preview.

Diver transitions retain the existing commands and autonomous work loop. A persisted descent record connects a brief kit check, entry and depth-dependent descent. Ascent uses actual depth; requested recovery draws the swimmer toward the working rail, lifts the net sack across the rail and places a short ladder climb inside the usual recovery time. The ladder moved from the decorative transom position to the actual port recovery area. Bodies use depth-dependent green absorption and contrast loss through the top of the water column, not an always-visible underwater marker. Bubbles begin after entry. Information entitlements remain separate from physical visibility.

The Pacific palette now uses dark green absorption and reduced seabed transparency, keeping shallow bottom/shadow detail. Kelp occupies localized stands independent of hidden catch grounds. Near-surface kelp and diver bodies use the same water-column optics. Reflection strength, texture directionality and noise were refined after independent criticism of the initial striped/glittering result.

Ownship has a screen-size contrast halo, vessel chevron and heading line; the full chart includes a direct position/heading label. Training prompts focus on the next action and define bow, stern and port in context; secondary material moves to optional notes. The volume control is a persistent native range input with keyboard, synthetic controller and touch coverage.

The reported clock-driven return was not reproduced: existing simulation only initiates ordinary homeward travel at the harbour boundary with the crew aboard. Multi-night tests now explicitly protect that agency, late warnings state that fishing may continue, and settlement uses the next actual shipping window, including the exact 06:00 boundary. Explicit rescue and catastrophic safety consequences remain.

## Review findings that changed the implementation

- Initial green water still formed regular diagonal rows and warm glitter. Reviewers rejected it; ripple fields and light response were revised.
- The pickup surface geometry rendered before the translucent water and disappeared. Its draw order now preserves it above the surface while respecting hull occlusion.
- A Realistic action prompt inferred underwater searching from exact-load visibility. Observability now follows the actual diver-information entitlement.
- The first fitting preview was too small and pushed benefits below the fold. Its framing, placement and purchase hierarchy were revised; cached snapshots avoid needless GPU readback on menu focus changes.
- The first fleet sheet exposed shared silhouettes and a phantom central bow rail on catamarans. Hull forms, liveries, cabin proportions and family details were revised; the twin-bow hardware now follows each hull.
- Compact recovery could show an obsolete completion message and a duplicate port callout over the active card. Stale nonurgent completion notices now expire with their operation, and redundant overlapping callouts defer to the readable card. Easy world labels avoid instrument panels and one another.
- The production matrix exposed wrapped crew text and an outdated Space-key fixture after volume became a slider. Crew cards now fit their actual content; native switch activation and native range input are tested separately.
- Independent visual inspection rejected two apparently passing galleries: the harbour class hid the canvas, then a traffic fixture omitted its interpolation accumulator. Capture scripts now select practice explicitly and verify visibility and finite traffic transforms. JSON mesh counts alone were not accepted as visual evidence.
- An independent CPU review reproduced three continuity faults: climbing before reaching the ladder from the outer pickup area, nightfall putting a still-preparing person into the sea, and descent snapping to a deeper bottom after current drift. The final climb now waits for actual arrival, preparation cancels safely while committed entry finishes, and descent follows the local seabed. The original repros and interrupted-catch/legacy-save checks were independently repeated successfully. The recovery estimate includes any remaining swim.

## References and preservation

All supplied vessel artwork stays intact under `public/assets/fleet`; new presentation geometry is procedural code. The original 2D project remains read-only and was not modified. BC visual study used the [Hakai Institute’s coastal kelp imagery and canopy explanation](https://hakai.org/stories/kelp-mapping-guidebook) and the [Kelp Rescue Initiative’s BC field imagery](https://www.kelprescue.org/). These are visual references, not redistributed game assets. Water visibility distances are the designer’s gameplay targets, not a real diving forecast.

## Verification

The final build passes 517 active tests with one intentional private-save skip, lint, formatting and production compilation. Eighteen Chromium suites pass: equipment, learning, diver operations, both boat galleries, interface, adversarial menus, accessibility, mobile career, HUD contexts, visual startup, keyboard/controller input, touch, vessel contracts, weather, natural voyage, embedded hosting and performance. The HUD fixture's final custom-panel restoration check now waits for the actual visible result instead of racing its two bounded render/cache passes with a fixed 100 ms sleep; its isolated rerun passes against the unchanged production build. The release receipt preserves both the initial matrix and this follow-up rather than presenting the initial matrix as failure-free.

The final natural voyage navigates to real ground, harvests and lands 300 lb, returns physically and settles with both divers fit and 100% hull. The hardware sample measures approximately 60 FPS at 1280×800, DPR 1, on AMD RADV VANGOGH over 240 live frames; this is a controlled scene sample. Independent reviewers opened the actual screenshot artifacts and recorded remaining limits. Safety-yellow shoulder panels and lighter tank paint improve two-metre contrast without changing physical scale or the five-metre extinction target. At the widest zoom people remain small; some traffic models intentionally share commercial family forms.
