# Bull kelp: floating stipe, air bulb, streamlined blades — October 5, 2026

Source: designer notes, October 5, and reference photographs `feedback/10-4 v2/region_nc_VanDamme_Bull-Kelp_2017.jpg`, `region_sea_feature01.jpg`, `header-exploding-kelp.jpg`, `OIP-3668431060.jpg`. Bible §2 vegetation entry updated in place, superseding the September 29/30 kelp appearance.

## What was wrong

Each plant was a short stipe topped by a bulb with seven wide, bright-yellow ribbons fanned up to ±1.15 m sideways. From the played top-down view the stands read as yellow feather sprays (`test-results/bull-kelp-2026-10-05/before/`). Floating stipe was barely visible even at low tide, unlike the 2D game and the photographs.

## Change

- **Shape (`src/three/marine-vegetation.js`).** Each plant has a 40-ring tapered stipe (radius 2.4 → 10 cm toward the bulb, so the floating part reads from above), one round air bulb, and four to six long (2.1–3.6 m), narrow (≤ 10 cm) blades. The blades leave the bulb together and trail downstream within ±0.04 rad, fanning to ±0.18 rad only as the current slackens. Colours are darker olive-brown.
- **Pose from tide and current, closed form in the vertex shader.**
  - The stipe rises from the fixed holdfast, leaning downstream with current (tan θ = 0.1 + 0.55 × extension).
  - Any length beyond the water depth floats along the surface: streamlined in current, loosely meandering at slack. The rising and floating sections blend smoothly at the waterline.
  - The bulb sits half-submerged at the end of the stipe. If the stipe cannot reach the surface (extreme tide or strong current), the bulb and blades stay under water and fade through the existing water-column optics.
  - Slow sway is a sine of time. There is no physics; each plant is one instanced shape (three variants), as before.
- **Lengths (`kelp-patches.js`).** Stipes are now water depth + 2.2–5.7 m. Coastal tides run about −0.5 to +2.8 m, so ordinary high water shows mainly bulbs and blades, while low water leaves 2.6–6 m of stipe afloat on every plant. Holdfasts, habitat depths (4–10 m) and independence from hidden catch are unchanged.
- **Heading and extension.** The existing per-plant easing remains: a few arithmetic operations per plant ten times a second, interpolated on the GPU. It keeps the September 30 behaviour of gathering at slack and swinging gradually at reversal without snapping. It is a filter on the baked current field, not a simulation.

## Evidence

- `scripts/bull-kelp-review.js` (suite `bull-kelp`) captures the same plants at tide −0.5 / +1.2 / +2.8 m in flood, ebb and slack, top-down at play zoom, wide, and in oblique close-ups. It asserts tide ordering and that at least 80% of plants keep their bulbs afloat at ordinary high water. Screenshots: `test-results/bull-kelp-2026-10-05/` (before images in `before/`).
- Cost at play zoom: 88 draw calls before and after; triangles fell from 529,112 to 515,696 (wide view: 799,680 → 777,160). The `performance` suite reports 59.93 FPS at 1280×800 on RADV VANGOGH, with draw p95 15.5 ms.
- Unit tests: tide exposure updated to the new lengths (ordinary high water ≥ 80% afloat, low water ≥ 2.5 m floating stipe on every plant). Root, flow and reversal tests are unchanged and pass. The September 29 `vegetation` fixture now allows −1 m and +2 m to both surface every plant; it stays strict for the extreme +5 m case and passes.

## Limits

Blades are flat ribbons with no translucency. The underwater stipe takes on the water-column green. Slack meanders are procedural, not true coils. Not yet seen on the designer's devices.
