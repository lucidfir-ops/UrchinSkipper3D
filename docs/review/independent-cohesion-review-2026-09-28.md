## What the screenshots substantiate

- **Equipment is more tangible.** The hauler is represented at the port working rail, selected fittings receive a visible gold location cue, and the preview changes from “Fitting preview” to “Your installed boat”. Purchase confirmation names the boat, installation area, exact effect, remaining cash and preservation of other fitted gear. The all-equipment preview does not look impossibly overcrowded.
- **All twelve boats render.** None of the twelve inspected fleet images is blank, replaced by an unrelated 2D asset, or visibly missing its basic hull/cabin/deck. Outboard, sterndrive, waterjet and twin-hull structures are distinguishable. This does not independently establish at-sea parity or collision geometry.
- **Depth is legible in the enlarged isolated fixture.** The diver is distinct at the surface, remains identifiable at 1–2 m, becomes a low-contrast green shape at 3.5 m and is absent at 5 m. The colour/contrast change reads as absorption in murky water, not a bright transparent ghost. Normal-zoom salience is weaker, as described above. A still-image sequence cannot prove the transition lacks a temporal pop; that needs motion/input testing.
- **The physical phase endpoints are coherent.** Preparing is at the working rail, entry is outside it, descent is submerged, boarding is adjacent to the ladder, and stowing returns the person to deck. The snapshots support physical staging; they cannot verify path continuity, timing, clipping between poses or interruption behaviour.
- **The added hauling endpoint ties catch to the rail.** In the regenerated `phase-hauling.png`, the diver is alongside the actual ladder/working rail and a red bag is visibly at that rail beside the existing deck sacks. This makes the transfer much more physical than a numerical load change. A single frozen frame does not prove its travel path or animation timing.
- **The water is substantially more appropriate to the requested coast.** In the inspected sea captures it is deep green, muted and opaque enough that the boat no longer sits over a tropical-looking clear seabed. Surface texture remains visible without obscuring the vessel. These captures do not establish shoreline/kelp/weather quality throughout the fifteen maps.
- **Ownship is easy to find.** Both full charts have a high-contrast gold vessel, enclosing ring, heading line and `YOU · 000°` label. It remains distinct from the green ground and pink rock symbol. The marker is prominent without dominating the chart. The minimap is also substantially clearer.
- **The slider reads as a slider.** Desktop and portrait captures show a clear track/thumb, percentage and keyboard guidance, with adequate surrounding spacing. Input responsiveness and persistence are reported by the associated test receipt, not independently tested here.
- **Tutorial tasks are narrower.** The first visible task requests a little ahead throttle and immediately defines bow as the front. The return task requests recovery and the south exit; shipping/economic advice lives in the chart note. This is a useful progressive-disclosure improvement. The actual ten-step learning curve still needs a new player's uninterrupted playthrough.
- **Easy and Realistic respect their visual distinction.** Easy retains both named status cards while underwater and in neutral. Realistic retains identity-only cards and generic bubble advice. Exact air/bag readouts in the Realistic context panel are permitted by the later §9/§18 amendments and are **not a defect**. An initial concern about these readouts was withdrawn after checking those amendments.
