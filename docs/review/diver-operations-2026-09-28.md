# Diver operations and coastal presentation review — September 28, 2026

## Implementation

The existing two diver entities and public simulation states remain. A saved transit record gives deployment a physical sequence: checking kit on the moving deck, entering from the port rail, then descending through the actual water column before searching or picking. Descent uses compressed game time of approximately ten metres per second after preparation and entry. Nitrox retains its extra preparation second. The existing five-second ascent warning now corresponds to a body rising through the column.

Requested, eligible recovery moves the swimmer through the water to the actual port ladder. Boat handling, relative-speed limits, collision consequences, catch conservation and the established bag/boarding durations remain. The last 0.85 seconds depict climbing and cannot start before the swimmer reaches the ladder; distant legal pickups can therefore take longer than the minimum timer. A subsequent 0.4-second walk to the assigned deck position does not delay commands. Immediate redeployment begins from that actual intermediate deck position. One bag command still exchanges the bag and sends the same eligible diver down when their orders, air, exposure and ground permit it.

The rendering contract is the pure `diverMotion` helper. It supplies position, height, heading, phase and progress without mutating simulation. Transit and deck arrival survive career serialization. Legacy deployment without a phase record retains its former surface-recovery exposure semantics; actual new submerged descent contributes exposure. Above-water kit preparation continues recovery.

The pickup guide now names the port ladder and explains that port is the left side. The quiet water patch follows the actual permitted sector. An inherited render-order problem had placed the guide beneath the water material; its outline and selected-diver ring now draw above the water while retaining depth testing. Rings stop at deck preparation, entry and boarding. Realistic guidance requires a genuinely visible nearby surface diver; Easy can also anticipate a known ascent.

## Inspection and refinements

The diver agent independently inspected the root agent's water/body rendering and the equipment agent's shop captures. The first water pass had conspicuous diagonal comb patterns and uniformly distributed gold glitter. The reviewed later water is substantially quieter, darker and greener. In enlarged depth captures, the diver's hood, tank and limbs remain readily identifiable at two metres; at 3.5 metres the figure becomes an indistinct green shape; at five metres it disappears. Entering and climbing use the physical port rail and ladder. A final follow-up also captures two metres at ordinary 1.1× and maximum playable 2.2× zoom, rather than relying exclusively on enlarged inspection images.

At the default 1.1× view the human figure remains physically small; bubbles continue to provide the strongest distant operational cue. The two-metre body is more readily recognizable at the normal maximum 2.2× zoom. These captures do not establish instant identification at every zoom and display size.

The initial equipment review found a tiny boat surrounded by excessive grid, hidden purchase benefits and confusing duplicate top-level Buy controls. Re-reviewed desktop hauler, portrait fuel-system and twinjet images address those findings: the boat occupies the preview, the location is marked, the measurable benefit precedes the image and the top action clearly opens the player's boat. Portrait still requires scrolling for secondary description, but the immediate purchase decision is visible.

Live touch review exposed the generic HELM reference obscuring recovery. The interface agent now suppresses only its default short-screen placement during an active recovery and restores it afterward, preserving the player's setting and custom layouts. This behavior is verified in the final diver browser suite.

A fresh independent critic additionally caught the port callout overlapping that same compact recovery card, and a twelve-second boarding completion notice contradicting a later phase of the named diver. The card now replaces the duplicate callout only when their actual rectangles overlap; the physical water guide remains. A nonurgent boarding completion notice clears when its named diver leaves the ready state. Urgent medical notices remain. The regression and browser checks cover both corrections.

The critic also found Easy world labels clipping beneath top instruments despite intact crew cards. Names now use the existing nearest-clear-space placement helper with a shared, bounded HUD obstacle cache refreshed at most ten times per second. The second label avoids the first; genuinely off-screen projected positions hide their world text and retain the existing delayed-arrow rules. Session reset clears placement and obstacle caches. A pure two-label regression passes, and the learning browser fixture now explicitly checks that both labels clear each other and visible core panels; the root's final targeted production follow-up owns the new screenshot verification.

Independent final simulation review found three edge cases. A far legal pickup with an empty bag could exhaust its minimum timer before reaching the ladder; both the timer and climbing pose now wait for ladder proximity. Darkness during deck preparation cancels with the diver aboard, while an already committed entry finishes before ascending. A current carrying a descending diver over a steep seabed slope previously caused a jump at the start of work; descent now follows the continuously sampled local bottom. Independent repros pass, including interrupted loaded recovery transferring exactly once and a legacy save without transit metadata.
