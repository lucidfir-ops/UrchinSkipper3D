# October 2 independent ocean presentation review

A reviewer separate from the implementation authors inspected the actual 1280×800 runtime images below with the image viewer. The final ocean-edge images were reopened after the exterior seabed continuation was added; the earlier intermediate captures were not used for final acceptance. This review concerns visible presentation, not collision geometry, physical controls or device performance.

Compared `test-results/feedback-2026/wind-15.png` with `test-results/wind-2026-10-02/wind-{0,5,15}.png` and `wind-15-close.png`. The old strong-wind view has conspicuous columns of repeated crescent marks. The replacement varies spacing, length and brightness, producing a substantially less mechanical surface. Zero wind has no visible whitecaps; wind 5 has sparse faint strokes, and wind 15 has a clearly stronger field. Boats, rocks and kelp remain readable. The kelp differences between the old and new captures are not attributed to this wind change.

The close strong-wind view still looks stylized: several crests are long, sharp dashed strokes with similar orientation, including a few approaching the boat's visible length. This is an honest remaining visual limitation, but the new field is a clear improvement over the repeated crescent pattern. Still images do not establish animation direction or pause behavior. The separate GPU probe receipt at `test-results/wind-2026-10-02-probe/receipt.json` records those checks; they were not independently rerun for this visual review.

Compared `test-results/ocean-edge-before/{wide-mean,east-low}.png` with the final `test-results/ocean-edge-2026-10-02/{wide-low,wide-mean,wide-high,east-low,south-mean}.png`. The old close east view visibly cuts from shallow green water to dark water along a straight line at the sector boundary. That hard line is absent in the final close view: the shallow color now continues smoothly across the dashed limit. The wide views at low, mean and high tide and the close southern view no longer show the abrupt rectangular water cutoff. Island shelves remain visible and change with tide; the sector markings and amber harbour exit remain legible.

A broad soft shallow-color halo remains perceptible around parts of the finite cove in the low-tide wide view. Bright kelp ribbons and shoreline foam still show procedural repetition, and the boat remains small at the widest zoom. These observations do not reveal a new blocking regression in the reviewed change. The captures support accepting this ocean presentation improvement; they do not establish all-map coverage, physics invariance, keyboard/controller/touch behavior, physical-device acceptance or completion of the game's wider requirements.

A second reviewer, who worked on calendar simulation rather than either ocean implementation, independently opened `test-results/ocean-edge-2026-10-02/east-low.png`, `wide-low.png`, `wide-high.png`, and `test-results/wind-2026-10-02/wind-15.png` plus `wind-15-close.png`. In the close east image, shallow color crosses the dashed sector line without a hard seam; both wide tide views also blend continuously into the surrounding water. The limit, harbour-facing amber line and nearby boat remain readable. Remaining flaws are visible: the low-tide cove has a soft pale halo, bright yellow kelp looks like repeated tangled strands, and Frank's wide-view card obscures the northern shoreline. The wide-view boat is recognizable but too small to inspect working details. In the wind captures, irregular spacing avoids obvious columns, but the close whitecaps still resemble sharp, mostly parallel scratches; several are roughly half the visible boat length. None of these images shows a blocking regression in the reviewed ocean changes. This second review establishes still-image legibility only, not motion or device performance.
