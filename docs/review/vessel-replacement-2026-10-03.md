# Lost-vessel replacement — October 3, 2026

Buying another copy of the currently lost hull could charge its full purchase price without restoring a usable boat. The purchase installed a fresh fleet record, then ordinary vessel selection synchronized the old live wreck into that same record. A replacement Harbour Workhorse therefore inherited zero hull health, damaged propulsion and the wreck's remaining fuel. The purchase returned success, but departure still said “Vessel lost.” Saving and reloading preserved the bad state.

The reproduction uses the normal new-career/starter transaction, stages a catastrophic loss, then runs real rescue, insurance, next-day and purchase APIs. Starting with $20,000, buying the $15,000 Workhorse leaves $5,000. Its insured loss and rescue leave $15,000 at the next harbour. Before this fix, spending that entire balance to replace the Workhorse leaves hull 0, drive 0.4 and 37 L aboard instead of a new vessel. This breaks the recovery path preserved by Bible §§13 and 15. It does not depend on a particular collision or a modified price.

`career-state.js` now separates applying the selected vessel from synchronizing the outgoing vessel. A purchase saves the outgoing state before replacing its fleet record, then applies the fresh vessel directly. Ordinary owned-vessel selection retains its synchronization, so selecting an already owned boat cannot provide a free refill or repair. Different-hull purchases retain the old wreck's record.

Direct Node verification passes four named regressions in `tests/vessel-replacement.test.js`: replacing each starter after rescue/reload and actually departing; buying a different replacement while preserving the old wreck and rejecting repeat refill/repair; and switching owned vessels after paid repairs while retaining separate fuel/damage. The existing nine career and fourteen second-video cases also pass. Focused ESLint and Prettier checks pass. Root owns full regression/build and production verification.
